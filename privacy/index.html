Privacy

BridgeX Privacy Policy
Updated October 16, 2026

Developer: Viacheslav Kuzmin.
Privacy questions and requests to delete information sent to support: support@techdatacloud.org.

BridgeX turns your iPhone or Android phone into a mouse and keyboard for your computer. The current version supports Mac; Windows support is in development. Remote control requires no account or external server. The developer does not receive commands, typed text, device names, pairing keys or usage history. The app contains no advertising, tracking or third-party analytics SDKs.

Local connection

Mouse, gesture and keyboard commands are encrypted and sent directly between your iPhone and Mac over Bluetooth LE or your home network. Other devices cannot control your Mac without pairing. Text entered into a Mac app is then handled by that app under its own policies.

Pairing and preferences

Device identifiers, paired-device names, keys and access tokens are stored locally in the system Keychain. The Mac also stores pairing dates and its list of trusted phones. Interface, sensitivity, gesture and connection preferences are stored locally in UserDefaults. The iPhone uses a random installation identifier to reconnect; no advertising identifier is used.

Typed text

BridgeX does not write typed text to disk or send it to the developer. The last 512 characters of the current typing session are held temporarily in iPhone memory so you can check for typos. The preview clears when you close the keyboard, leave the app for the background or disconnect. It does not read existing Mac field contents or record the screen.

Permissions

The iPhone camera reads pairing QR codes locally; images are not saved. You can enter a pairing code manually instead. Bluetooth and local-network access are used to discover and connect your Mac. Accessibility permission on the Mac allows mouse and keyboard commands to run. You can revoke these permissions in system settings.

Deletion and revocation

Choose Forget Mac in iPhone settings to remove its stored Mac pairing. Choose Revoke access beside the phone on your Mac to remove that phone from the trusted list and close its active connection. Do both to remove pairing data from both devices. The random installation identifier and host identity may remain in Keychain after app deletion; uninstalling alone does not guarantee Keychain cleanup.

Support and external pages

The app does not automatically send diagnostic reports. If you choose to contact support, the information you provide is used to respond. Do not send pairing codes, keys or passwords. When you open a support or privacy page in a browser, the website and its hosting provider may receive ordinary technical HTTP request information; BridgeX does not send the contents of your remote-control session to them.

Android app

This policy also applies to BridgeX for Android. Android 12 or later is supported. Commands and pairing identifiers are encrypted and sent directly between your Android device and your paired Mac; the developer cannot read these transfers and receives no session data. There are no BridgeX accounts, ads or analytics SDKs.

Android storage and text

Android stores a random installation identifier and preferences in app-private SharedPreferences. Pairing keys and credentials are encrypted with an Android Keystore key before they are saved. App backup and device transfer of these records are disabled. The optional typing preview holds at most 500 Unicode code points in memory. It clears when hidden or cleared, on disconnect, loss of control permission, or when the app goes to the background. It does not read existing text from your Mac. Your selected Android keyboard processes input under its own privacy policy; BridgeX requests that the keyboard not use this field for personalized learning, but cannot guarantee another keyboard’s behavior.

Android permissions and deletion

Camera permission is optional for local QR scanning; frames are not saved or uploaded. Nearby devices permission is used for Bluetooth discovery and connection, never to derive location. Network access discovers your Mac and carries encrypted local commands. Android does not use an Accessibility service. On the Mac, Accessibility permission is needed for remote input. Choose Forget this Mac on Android and revoke the phone on your Mac to remove the pairing from both devices. Uninstalling Android removes its local app data; it does not revoke the trusted record on your Mac. Opening support or website links is optional and uses your chosen browser or mail app. Google Play and your operating system may handle their own diagnostics under their own policies.

Children and families
BridgeX is intended for computer users aged 6 and above, including families. It does not ask for a child’s name, age, email, location or advertising identifier. There are no ads, purchases, public profiles, chat, or user-generated content feeds. A parent or guardian should supervise device pairing and the content opened on the controlled computer. Optional QR camera frames stay on the phone. If a child contacts support, a parent or guardian can request deletion of that support correspondence at support@techdatacloud.org.

Google Play and device services

Google Play distributes and signs Android builds and may verify their installation source. Google and the operating system process store, licensing and diagnostic information under their own policies; Play Console can provide the developer with aggregate installation and technical performance reports. BridgeX does not send your typed text, pairing keys or remote-control session to Google. No BridgeX advertising or analytics SDK is integrated.
